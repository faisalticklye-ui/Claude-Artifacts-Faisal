Middle East

Israel says it foiled West Bank "terror attack" near Hebron

Troops killed two gunmen in an attack the army called targeted at Israeli civilians; the prime minister's office ordered preparations for a full closure of the West Bank ahead of Tuesday's election.

Gunmen open fire on a church in Jordan's Karak

Attackers fired on worshippers in Karak, about 180km south of Amman, killing and wounding several; no group had claimed responsibility as authorities investigated.

Houthi strikes on Saudi oil sites send crude to five-month high

Attacks on Saudi Arabia's oil infrastructure stoked fresh supply fears, with Brent topping $116 a barrel and WTI pushing above $100 amid broader Strait of Hormuz tensions.

UNICEF: over 350 children killed in Gaza since October 2025 deal

The agency said child deaths have continued despite last year's ceasefire agreement, while the World Food Programme cut its aid caseload from 1.6 million to 1.1 million people amid bread shortages.

India

Infosys reports September-quarter results, dividend decision

The IT bellwether announced its Q2 FY27 earnings and a dividend call Friday, with the stock in focus through the session as investors weighed the numbers against guidance.

Modi accuses Congress of courting "disorder" amid global shift

Prime Minister Narendra Modi told the NDTV World Summit that the opposition Congress party "dreams of disorder" while the world seeks a new order, and called for reform of multilateral institutions to give the Global South a greater voice.

Sensex, Nifty close lower for a fourth straight session

Benchmark indices slipped Friday as surging crude oil prices and weakness in metal, energy and financial stocks weighed on sentiment; the volatility index rose about 6%.

Poland seeks deeper defence-export ties with India

Warsaw signalled interest in expanding defence exports and industrial cooperation with India, part of broader efforts between the two countries to boost bilateral trade.

Europe

EU leaders join Budapest's 1956 uprising anniversary

Ursula von der Leyen and António Costa attended Budapest's 70th-anniversary commemoration, part of a broader diplomatic thaw with Hungary as the country's political mood shifts.

Macron bars protests not organised by unions

France's main student union condemned the move as an attack on the right to protest, after the president restricted demonstrations to union-organised events.

About 150,000 rally in Belgrade against Vučić

Crowds marched through the Serbian capital ahead of Sunday's parliamentary election, in one of the largest demonstrations against President Aleksandar Vučić in years.

Polish minister resigns amid coalition rift

Development and regional-funds minister Katarzyna Pełczyńska-Nałęcz stepped down after a dispute within Donald Tusk's coalition over term limits for local authorities.

USA

Wall Street closes a rocky week lower on AI-spending jitters

The Nasdaq Composite slipped to 25,955.34 (−0.21%) Friday, extending a week dominated by doubts over heavy AI-infrastructure spending after Thursday's steep tech-led selloff.

DOJ charges Virginia Senate leader with racketeering

Federal prosecutors charged Virginia state Senate majority leader Louise Lucas with racketeering and money-laundering conspiracy tied to a cannabis business she co-owns with family.

Trump administration weighing new strikes on Iran, officials say

U.S. Ambassador Mike Waltz told the Financial Times the standoff with Tehran could last another year, as tensions over the Strait of Hormuz continued.

ICE agents filmed attempting to take toddlers from father in Rhode Island

Footage from Providence showed agents trying to take two young children from their Guatemalan father, drawing fresh scrutiny of immigration-enforcement tactics.
